Page 2 · 10 minutes · Meet NEW

What is the Next Education Workforce?

Most schools are organized around one teacher, one classroom: you get your own list of students (your roster), and those students are yours for the year. NEW asks a different question: what if a team of educators shared one larger roster of students instead?

Conventional model

1 teacher · 1 classroom · about 25 students

You plan, teach, assess, and support your 25 students largely on your own. The teacher next door has their own 25.

Team-based model

A team of educators · several classrooms · one shared roster

For example, three teachers might share responsibility for all 75 students across their three rooms. Together, they decide who teaches which students, when, and how.

The key shift is the roster, not the room. In a team-based model, every student on the shared roster belongs to the whole team, not to one teacher. Students may move between educators and spaces during the day: one teacher might lead a small group that needs extra practice, while another works with a larger group ready to go further, and a third gives one-on-one feedback. The groups change as students' needs change.

Is this just co-teaching?

Not quite. In co-teaching, two teachers usually share one class in one room. A NEW team may sometimes co-teach, with two educators in the same space for a lesson, but that's just one option they can choose. What defines the model is that the team shares responsibility for a larger group of students across classrooms and organizes its time, spaces, and expertise around what those students need.

Teams can also include other adults with different roles, such as paraeducators, student teachers, specialists, or community educators, so each person can contribute what they do best. Teams look different from school to school.
